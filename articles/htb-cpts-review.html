01 Introduction: Scope and Rigor of the HTB CPTS

Attainment of the Hack The Box Certified Penetration Testing Specialist (HTB CPTS) certification represents a significant benchmark in professional offensive security assessment. Unlike examinations that evaluate isolated exploit primitives or impose artificial 24-hour constraints, the CPTS examination is structured to verify competency in conducting a comprehensive enterprise penetration test.

The assessment does not utilize isolated capture-the-flag machines or artificial hints. Instead, the candidate operates against an interconnected corporate infrastructure in which every credential, configuration file, service ticket, and network route is operationally relevant. Successful completion requires both technical execution across web, Linux, Windows, and Active Directory environments—achieving a strict minimum score of 85 out of 100 marks—and the submission of a commercial-grade penetration testing report.

02 18-Week Preparation: The 28-Module (44d 2h) Path and CherryTree Checklists

Eligibility for the CPTS examination voucher requires 100% completion of the Penetration Tester Job-Role Path on HTB Academy. This curriculum consists of 28 technical modules with an official estimated duration of 44 days and 2 hours.

Passive review of the instructional material is insufficient for examination readiness. Encompassing the completion of all 28 training modules, execution of all practical skill assessments, laboratory practice, and analysis of IppSec's Unofficial CPTS machine series, the preparation phase required 18 weeks of sustained study.

Structured Documentation with CherryTree and Custom Checklists

Throughout the 44 days and 2 hours of curriculum instruction, CherryTree served as the primary hierarchical documentation application. A tree-structured repository facilitated the organization of each module by network service, port number, and exploitation phase.

Crucially, each practitioner must develop an independent operational checklist. Reliance on external reference sheets is ineffective when analyzing an unfamiliar internal subnet during the examination. Within CherryTree, verified checklists were maintained for the following domains:

  • External and Web Enumeration Checklist: Port scanning, virtual host and subdomain discovery (ffuf), directory enumeration, parameter manipulation, Local and Remote File Inclusion (LFI/RFI), SQL Injection (SQLi), file upload filter bypasses, and content management system analysis.
  • Pivoting and Internal Routing Checklist: Verified syntax for configuring ligolo-ng TUN interfaces, single and multi-hop relay listeners, and internal port forwarding rules.
  • Active Directory Checklist: Unauthenticated enumeration, BloodHound data collection, Kerberoasting, AS-REP Roasting, password spraying, SMB and RPC share analysis, Access Control List (ACL/ACE) exploitation (GenericAll, WriteDacl, ForceChangePassword), Kerberos Delegation, and Domain Trust exploitation.
  • Post-Exploitation and Privilege Escalation Checklist: Systematic credential harvesting across compromised Linux and Windows hosts, including configuration files, databases, PowerShell command history, scheduled tasks, registry keys, SUID binaries, Linux capabilities, and Windows access token privileges such as SeImpersonatePrivilege.

03 IppSec's Unofficial CPTS Series and Distinctions from AEN

Completing exercises within an HTB Academy module provides inherent contextual indicators regarding the underlying vulnerability class. To prepare for an unassisted black-box assessment, substantial preparation time was dedicated to IppSec's Unofficial CPTS Preparation Playlist and the ippsec.rocks indexing platform.

IppSec has curated a selection of Hack The Box machines that align with the analytical rigor required for the CPTS examination. Reviewing these technical walkthroughs following independent machine completion illustrates diverse enumeration methodologies, rapid hypothesis validation, recovery procedures following failed exploitation attempts, and identification of subtle misconfigurations omitted by automated scanners.

Critical Assessment Distinction: AEN Does Not Replicate the Examination
A prevalent misconception within the candidate community is equating the final Academy module—Attacking Enterprise Networks (AEN)—with the CPTS examination. AEN does not replicate the examination. While AEN provides valuable practice in chaining discrete techniques, the actual CPTS examination differs substantially and presents a significantly higher degree of difficulty. Completion of AEN with contextual guidance does not guarantee examination readiness; candidates must anticipate deeper enumeration requirements, complex dead ends, and multi-hop exploitation chains during the formal assessment.

04 Examination Structure: Black-Box Enterprise Simulation

The HTB CPTS examination is engineered to simulate a commercial black-box penetration test in which the candidate serves as the lead assessor.

At the commencement of the assessment, the candidate receives a formal Letter of Engagement and Scope specification. Initial access data is restricted to a single external IP address and a domain name; the operational objective is to progress from the external perimeter into the core internal corporate network.

  • External-to-Internal Progression: Starting from the designated external IP address and domain name, the assessor must enumerate public-facing web applications and network services, breach the perimeter, identify dual-homed network interfaces, and route traffic into isolated internal subnets.
  • 10-Day Assessment Window: A total duration of 10 consecutive days is allocated to execute the technical penetration test and submit the final report.
  • Passing Threshold (Mandatory Standard): The examination environment contains 14 flags valued at a total of 100 marks. Candidates must achieve a minimum score of 85 out of 100 marks (corresponding to at least 12 flags) and submit a comprehensive, commercial-grade technical report that satisfies HTB's evaluation standards. Attainment of the point threshold without an acceptable technical report results in failure.

Analysis of October 26–28 and Flag 8: The Core Principle
Experiencing three consecutive days (10-26, 10-27, and 10-28) without acquiring a flag following rapid initial progression presents a substantial analytical challenge. Flag 8 represented the most difficult objective of the assessment. Resolution of this impasse on 10-29 resulted from adherence to the Core Methodological Principle: When progression stalls, re-execute baseline enumeration from the beginning. Every compromised host, configuration file, and checklist item must be systematically re-examined to identify previously overlooked artifacts.

06 Commercial Reporting via SysReptor and Examiner Feedback

Acquiring 12 flags (85 points) fulfills only the technical prerequisite of the assessment; the written deliverable is evaluated as a formal commercial engagement report. To produce a structured, commercial-grade document efficiently, SysReptor was utilized in conjunction with the reporting framework documented in Bruno Rocha Moura's Road to CPTS.

Adherence to SysReptor's CPTS template and established reporting standards—specifically preserving third-party auditor independence in remediation guidance and articulating business impact—contributed directly to the official evaluation outcome.

07 Methodological Principles, Checklists, and Core Guidelines

The following operational principles governed the 18-week preparation phase and the October 23 – November 2 examination engagement:

#01 Core Principle: Re-Enumerate When Stalled

When encountering a multi-day impasse (such as the October 26–28 period preceding Flag 8), cease speculative exploitation. Return to baseline and re-execute enumeration procedures systematically; the required vector typically resides within a previously overlooked service, share, or configuration file.

#02 Minimum 85/100 Marks and Technical Report

The minimum passing score is strictly 85 out of 100 marks (12 flags). While flag acquisition is mandatory, certification is contingent upon the submission of a thorough, commercial-grade technical report.

#03 Develop Independent Checklists in CherryTree

Utilize CherryTree throughout the 44-day, 2-hour Academy curriculum to construct structured checklists categorized by service and attack phase. Maintaining verified command syntax conserves substantial time during the examination.

#04 Recognize That AEN Does Not Replicate the Exam

Treat Attacking Enterprise Networks (AEN) as foundational practice rather than a replica of the final assessment. The CPTS examination presents substantially greater complexity and should be supplemented with IppSec's Unofficial CPTS machine series.

#05 Utilize SysReptor and Established Reporting Standards

Configure SysReptor prior to Day 1 and adhere to the reporting methodology outlined in Bruno Rocha Moura's guide. Formulate vendor-neutral remediation advice to preserve third-party auditor independence.

#06 Provide Descriptive Captions for All Evidence

In accordance with official examiner recommendations, include descriptive captions for all command output blocks and photographic evidence so engineering personnel can reproduce each finding directly from the report.

Conclusion
Completing the 28 curriculum modules (44 days, 2 hours), conducting 18 weeks of practical preparation across laboratory environments and IppSec's Unofficial CPTS series, resolving the three-day Flag 8 impasse during the October 23 – November 2 examination window, satisfying the mandatory 85/100 scoring threshold, and receiving commendation from the examining board validated the rigor of this certification. For practitioners seeking an assessment that evaluates end-to-end enterprise penetration testing from an external IP address to full internal domain compromise, the HTB CPTS represents a premier industry standard.
